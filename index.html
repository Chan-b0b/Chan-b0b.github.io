I also conducted visiting research at Drexel University, USA (funded by KIAT), working on 3D reconstruction and RL-based view planning.

My research interests span reinforcement learning, imitation learning, VLA/VLM models, and humanoid robot control — with a strong emphasis on bridging the gap between simulation and real-world deployment.

selected publications

  1. IEEE Access
    Dynamic-Persistent CSMA: A Reinforcement Learning Approach for Multi-User Channel Access
    Chanho Lee, Seungkeun Park, and Taesu Cheong
    IEEE Access, Nov 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This paper addresses the challenge of multiple users concurrently sharing a single channel in a wireless network. We propose Dynamic-Persistent CSMA (DP-CSMA), incorporating a deep reinforcement learning model that dynamically adjusts the waiting period based on current state. The DRL model captures dynamic history using LSTM and compresses actions into skills via a Variational Autoencoder, enabling zero-shot generalization: trained on 5–10 agents, the policy achieves top throughput even in 100-agent environments without retraining.

  2. JAIRA
    Is View Planning Always Effective for 3D Reconstruction?
    Chanho Lee and David Han
    Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research and Applications, Nov 2025
    Abs Bib

    We investigate how the effectiveness of view planning varies with a reconstruction model’s dependency on semantic information. We introduce PIFu-A, a pixel-aligned implicit function model with an attention-based encoder for multi-view 3D reconstruction, and compare it with Pix2Vox. Using an RL-based (PPO) next-best-view planning architecture, we show that view planning is significantly more effective for geometric models than semantic ones—up to 80% improvement—validated on ShapeNet (39,699 models, 13 categories). Research conducted at Drexel University, USA.

  3. PhysRev
    Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?
    A. Einstein*†, B. Podolsky*, and N. Rosen*
    Phys. Rev., New Jersey. More Information can be found here , May 1935
    Abs DOI HTML PDF Video

    In a complete theory there is an element corresponding to each element of reality. A sufficient condition for the reality of a physical quantity is the possibility of predicting it with certainty, without disturbing the system. In quantum mechanics in the case of two physical quantities described by non-commuting operators, the knowledge of one precludes the knowledge of the other. Then either (1) the description of reality given by the wave function in quantum mechanics is not complete or (2) these two quantities cannot have simultaneous reality. Consideration of the problem of making predictions concerning a system on the basis of measurements made on another system that had previously interacted with it leads to the result that if (1) is false then (2) is also false. One is thus led to conclude that the description of reality as given by a wave function is not complete.
